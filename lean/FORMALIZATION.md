# Mathematical scope

## Classical linear bound

The main open target is a theorem about `WaringBound` asserting that every sufficiently large positive integer is a sum of at most the stated number of positive powers. Its number-field counterpart is `WaringSiegelBound`, with the power-order eligibility, positivity and norm conditions in `WaringSiegel.NumberFieldTargets`. The formal results below establish several parts of these proofs.

The module `PrimeCountingChebyshev` proves explicit two-sided Chebyshev bounds for the actual prime-counting function. `PrimeWindowBounds` proves two-sided reciprocal-mass bounds for the power windows used by the method. `MarkedMass` identifies the cardinality of the distinct marked support with the sum of the cofactor floors and gives a positive lower density. `MarkedChildren` identifies the exact support after dividing by any group of marks, including its cofactor size and smoothness. `MarkedSelection` verifies the greedy group choice at the new scale. `MarkedSmoothDensity` constructs the exponent windows and proves positive density at every fixed positive smoothness exponent. `PrimeWindowLimit` gives the exact limiting window mass from its stated prime-number-theorem hypothesis.

The modules `TraceConductorStage` and `ScalarConductorStage` prove the conductor moment update for actual trace Weyl sums and actual positive integer supports under the stated fibre estimates. `ScalarShortening` discharges the rational small-modulus and fibre estimates in the classical moment step. A proof of the sharp linear representation theorem still requires the initial restricted moment estimate, the genuine analytic update on the smooth sums across the conductor ranges, and unconditional major-arc lower bounds for the relevant mixed sums. The classical finite computations for degrees 1 through 20 also give the exact ratios of the separate historical bounds listed in the manuscript.

`RankTwoPacking` proves the determinant packing bound for finite sets in an integral lattice by an exact quotient-and-floor argument. `FieldRankTwo` specializes it to the number-field Minkowski shear and `FieldRankOne` gives the complementary common-slope implication. `PositiveUnitBalancing` derives the required bounded rescaling by powers of positive units from the Dirichlet unit lattice. `FieldDifferencingOrder` obtains the sharp starting seed at the scheduled order from actual algebraic-integer power-sum equality counts satisfying the stated differencing inequality.

`WaringLinear.waring_siegel_max_of_primitive_width` proves the actual global representation bound with threshold max(L, floor(6.873k)) for k at least 2501. Its local premise is a unit-coordinate representation of each eligible element in every completed integer ring in L variables. The analytic premises are the efficient-differencing power-equality count, the marked conductor update, ordinary broad-arc estimates and positive core and annular/minor estimates for the marked sums on locally soluble targets. The proof derives the loss-free moment and the outer error, pads local solutions and extracts bounded positive roots from the Fourier coefficient.

The module `LeastPrimitiveWidth` proves, without analytic assumptions, that the least simultaneous primitive width over all completed integer rings exists in every field at degrees at least three. It gives the uniform elementary upper bound of eight times the fifth power of the degree, and proves the defining minimality.

The finite field certificate uses 393 schedules covering every integer degree from 11 through 2500. The conservative rounded recurrence, the efficient-differencing initial defect and the terminal fees are checked by the Lean kernel. The 2,260 distinct real conductor certificates used in this range pass the finite checker. The association of every run with its conductor certificate is verified in the accompanying numerical calculation. The corresponding join is a premise of the formal theorem between degrees 21 and 2500. The earlier certificates and the logarithmic price calculation cover the unbounded range.

The historical mean-value theorem over arbitrary number fields requires the analytic differencing estimate in addition to its ideal arithmetic and recurrence. The field linear bound further requires the conductor descent and the complete analysis of the minor and major arcs on the trace torus.

## Local results

* `SquareZeroUnits`, `QuotientUnitCorrections` and `DeviationBasis` prove additivity and compression of deviations of actual power units in a square-zero layer. `PrincipalUnitWidths` derives equality of ordinary and primitive widths under explicit principal-unit lifting and nonunit-power hypotheses.
* `RamifiedCubicIntegers` identifies the ring of integral elements of the quadratic field generated over the 3-adics by a square root of 6. `RamifiedCubicOrder` proves the exact three-, four- and five-cube criteria in this ring, with 17, 23 and 27 eligible residue classes modulo 9. `CubicLocalLifting` gives a general adic lifting lemma and the five-cube identity over local rings with invertible 2.
* `WaringLocal.QuadraticEisensteinOrder` identifies the order in a ramified quadratic local field for every radicand of valuation one. `SqrtTwoWidths` and `SqrtTwoSmallWidths` prove exact ordinary and primitive widths in the dyadic square-root-of-two field, including 7 for quartics, 17 for octics and four times the dyadic part of every degree divisible by four from 12 onward.
* `TeichCarry` proves existence of a three-term Teichmüller sum of valuation one modulo the square of an odd prime. `LocalWordCost` proves the weighted finite-module inequalities used in odd residue characteristic. `DyadicObstruction` and `DyadicDensityObstruction` include the classical local obstruction at powers of two from degree eight onward.

These local endpoints concern specified fields or rings. The general asymptotic theorem for local widths also needs the full ramification and residue-degree argument across all places.

## Earlier global endpoints

The module `CubicImaginary` proves seven positive cubes over every totally imaginary number field, including the norm bound on the individual roots. `CubicFermat` proves six cubes under an integral tangent-point hypothesis, with corollaries for totally imaginary fields containing a primitive ninth root of unity or a cube root of two. Its proof uses the archimedean rounding, finite dyadic cube-span and ternary pairing results.

The module `ClassicalInitialBounds` proves the literal classical positive-representation bounds of one for degree one, four for squares and eight for cubes.

The modules `WSL4MajorTarget`, `WSL4PolynomialTarget`, `WSL4ArchOddPrimeTarget` and `WSL4EightCubesTarget` give the earlier number-field theorems. They include the uniform polynomial bound of eight times the fifth power of the exponent, and eight positive cubes over every number field. `WaringPrior.Global` proves the quartic bound of 16 and the octic bound of 61 under the stated dyadic unramifiedness conditions. The quartic bound is 15 when each dyadic integer ring also contains a root of the third cyclotomic polynomial.

The module `SmoothSeed` derives an unconditional quantitative seed for classical smooth Weyl sums in every integer degree at least two. A normalized defect D is available at half-order two plus the ceiling of minus k log D. `SmoothSeedSubsets` transfers this bound to every smooth subset. `GeometricLinearOrders` gives a linear moment order below 8.873 times the degree plus four from this unconditional seed, conditional on the new shortening step and transfer to the selected family. The refined `scalar_linear_orders_from_smooth_seed` uses the actual scheduled heights to give a bound below 8.183 times the degree plus six for Fourier moments on every eligible smooth support. Its premises are the two shortening rules on that family. The geometric seed, the transfer and finite initial scales are all proved.
